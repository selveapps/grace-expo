# Grace — Tea Scripts  
**Voice Actor Edition**  
*For TestFlight / App Distribution*  
*Style: 19–22 y/o Gen-Z girl sending a chaotic 60–75 second voice note to her best friend at 1 a.m.*

---

### Performance Guide (Read This First)

**Overall energy**  
- You are not narrating. You are reacting in real time.  
- Talk like you’re texting with your mouth.  
- Speed up when the story gets crazy.  
- Slow down and drop your voice on the emotional punches.  
- Laugh when something is absurd (short, natural “ha” or breathy laugh — never forced).  
- Use vocal fry and upward inflection on the hooks.  
- End most scripts with a soft, almost whispered final line or a quiet “I’m obsessed / I’m soft / I can’t”.

**Notation Key**  
- **BOLD** = stress / punch the word  
- *italics* = softer, almost throwaway  
- [pause] = 0.5–1 second breath  
- [long pause] = 1.5–2 seconds  
- [laugh] = short natural laugh or amused exhale  
- [soft] = drop volume + slow down  
- [fast] = speed up, almost tripping over words  
- [whisper] = very quiet, intimate  
- ↑ = upward inflection (like a question even if it’s not)

## 1. Vashti Said No  
`vashti-no` · Esther 1 · ~68s

**Hook (vocal fry + upward energy):**  
Okay bestie I need you to sit **all the way down** for this one… [pause]

This king owns **127** provinces and throws a seven-day party so massive the whole city is invited. Day seven he’s wasted and decides he wants his wife to walk out in the crown so every man can **stare** at her.  

Meanwhile she’s throwing her **own** feast for the women in the same palace.  

She says **no**. [pause]  
No explanation. Just **no**.  

He doesn’t even go talk to her. He calls an emergency meeting of advisors like this is a **national security threat**. One of them stands up and basically says, “If the other women hear about this they’re all gonna start disrespecting their husbands.”  

So they pass a **permanent** law: every man must rule his own house. Letters go out in every language of the empire.  

[soft] One woman said no at a dinner party…  
and an entire empire had a complete meltdown.  

[whisper] I’m obsessed with her.

## 2. Loyalty in the Barley  
`ruth-field` · Ruth 2 · ~70s

**Hook:**  
No because why is this the most **soft launch** of a dynasty I’ve ever seen… [pause]

Naomi loses her husband and both her sons. She’s left with two foreign daughters-in-law and tells them both to go home because she has **nothing** left to give.  

One leaves.  

The other one, Ruth, looks her dead in the eye and says “where you go I go, your people my people, your God my God.” That’s not cute. That’s a **lifelong vow**.  

They walk into Bethlehem broke during harvest. Ruth starts gleaning — literally picking up other people’s leftovers in the field.  

And somehow she “happens” to land in the field of a rich relative named Boaz. He notices her immediately, tells his men not to touch her, then quietly tells them to drop **extra** grain for her on purpose.  

She goes home with an **insane** amount of barley.  

Fast forward: he marries her, they have a son, and that son’s grandson is **King David**.  

[soft] Broke foreign widow picking up scraps… three generations later her bloodline is on the throne.  

[whisper] I can’t.

## 3. Court Under a Palm  
`deborah-palm` · Judges 4 · ~65s

**Hook:**  
Wait wait wait… a woman was literally the **judge** of the whole nation and nobody talks about it enough… [pause]

Deborah.  

The text doesn’t say she “helped” or “advised.” It says she **judged** Israel. She held court under a palm tree and people came to her for actual legal decisions.  

She summons the military commander Barak and gives him God’s battle plan.  

This grown man looks at her and says, “I’ll only go if **you** come with me.”  

She goes… but she tells him to his face: “This victory won’t be yours. The Lord is going to hand the enemy over to a **woman**.”  

And that’s exactly what happens. Sisera ends up dead in a tent with a hammer through his head.  

So the official record is: the prophetess gave the orders, the general needed a babysitter, and another woman finished the job.  

[soft] Palm tree. Entire nation.  

[whisper] I’m screaming.

## 4. She Rode Straight at 400 Swords  
`abigail-intercept` · 1 Samuel 25 · ~72s

**Hook:**  
Girl the way she pulled up on **400** armed men like it was nothing… [pause]

David is about to commit a massacre. Some rich guy named Nabal just insulted him after David’s men protected his sheep for free, so David straps on his sword and takes 400 men. He’s going to kill every male in the house.  

A servant runs to Nabal’s wife Abigail like “your husband just signed our death warrant.”  

She doesn’t cry. She does **inventory**. Loads up donkeys with food and wine and sends them ahead. Doesn’t even tell her husband.  

Then she rides out herself, meets 400 armed men on the road, and starts talking. Calls her own husband a fool to David’s face. Talks the future king completely down. The 400 men turn around.  

She goes home, waits until her husband is sober the next morning, tells him what she did… and he has a stroke basically. Ten days later he’s dead.  

David hears about it and sends for her.  

She becomes his wife.  

[soft] Girl said “not today” to 400 swords and a future king… and won.

## 5. The Most Costly Yes  
`mary-yes` · Luke 1 · ~68s

**Hook:**  
Bestie she said yes to a plan that could’ve gotten her **killed** and I’m still not over it… [pause]

Gabriel shows up in a random village nobody cares about and tells a girl who’s engaged (not married) that she’s about to get pregnant by the Holy Spirit and her son will be the forever king.  

She’s troubled by the **greeting** first, not even the angel. Then when the actual news lands her only question is “how, since I’ve never been with a man?”  

She’s not asking for proof. She’s asking **logistics**.  

In her village this could ruin her life or get her killed. And she still says yes. “Be it unto me according to thy word.”  

[soft] Yes to a plan she couldn’t verify, in a town that could count.  

[whisper] I’m not okay.

## 6. The Better Part  
`martha-mary` · Luke 10 · ~65s

**Hook:**  
No because Jesus really said her name **twice** like he was talking to his favorite person… [pause]

Jesus walks into a house. Martha is the host and she’s drowning in the work — cooking, serving, stressed. Her sister Mary is just sitting at Jesus’ feet listening.  

Martha finally snaps and goes over Mary’s head: “Lord, don’t you care that my sister left me to do everything alone? Tell her to help me.”  

She’s not asking. She’s **ordering** him.  

And instead of scolding her he says her name twice — “Martha, Martha” — the way you talk to someone you love who’s spiraling.  

Then he tells her she’s worried about too many things, and Mary chose the one thing that can’t be taken from her.  

He never tells Mary to get up. He never says the serving was wrong.  

[soft] He basically tells Martha she’s allowed to sit down too.  

[whisper] I’m soft for that.

## 7. She Left the Waterpot  
`well-woman` · John 4 · ~70s

**Hook:**  
Okay the first person Jesus fully revealed himself to was a woman with **five exes** and I’m screaming… [pause]

Jesus is tired and sits by a well at the hottest part of the day. A Samaritan woman comes to draw water. He asks her for a drink (already scandalous) and the conversation goes sideways fast.  

He offers her living water. She wants it. He says go call your husband. She says she doesn’t have one.  

He goes, “You’re right… you’ve had **five**. And the one you have now isn’t your husband.”  

He’s not shaming her. He’s telling her he already knew every messy detail and he’s still sitting there talking to her.  

Then she brings up the Messiah and he looks at her and says “I that speak unto thee am he.”  

First time in the whole Gospel he says it that clearly… to **her**.  

She leaves her waterpot. Just abandons it. Runs back to town and becomes the first evangelist.  

[soft] Five exes and a waterpot.  

[whisper] I’m obsessed.

## 8. If I Perish  
`esther-uninvited` · Esther 4 · ~68s

**Hook:**  
She walked in knowing it could **kill** her and still said “if I perish, I perish”… [pause]

There’s a law in Persia: if you walk into the king’s presence without being summoned, you die. Unless he holds out the golden scepter.  

Esther tells Mordecai she hasn’t been called in **thirty days**. She’s the queen and she has no idea if she’s still safe.  

Mordecai hits her with the hardest truth: don’t think the palace will protect you. If you stay silent, deliverance will come from somewhere else… but you and your family might not make it.  

Then he drops the line: “Who knows if you came to the kingdom for such a time as this?”  

She fasts three days and sends back four words: “If I perish, I perish.”  

Third day she walks in. The king holds out the scepter.  

She lives… and then throws the dinner party that takes down the second most powerful man in the empire.  

[soft] I’m obsessed with her courage.

## 9. Only Her Lips Moved  
`hannah-prayer` · 1 Samuel 1 · ~68s

**Hook:**  
The priest really called her **drunk** for praying too hard and I’m not over it… [pause]

Hannah can’t have kids. Her husband’s other wife has plenty and provokes her about it every single year at the place of worship.  

Hannah stops eating and just weeps. Her husband tries to comfort her with the most well-meaning terrible line ever: “Am I not better to you than ten sons?”  

She gets up, goes to the temple, and prays so hard that only her **lips** are moving. No sound.  

The priest watches her and decides she’s drunk. Out loud. “How long will you be drunk? Put away your wine.”  

She doesn’t shrink. She just says, “No my lord, I’m a woman of a sorrowful spirit. I haven’t had anything to drink. I’ve been pouring out my soul before the Lord.”  

Most honest prayer in the building and the priest called her a drunk.  

She walks out still childless… but the text says her face wasn’t sad anymore.  

[whisper] I’m soft for her.

## 10. The First to See  
`magdalene-first` · John 20 · ~65s

**Hook:**  
Peter and John left… she **stayed**… and that’s why she saw Him first… [pause]

Mary Magdalene gets to the tomb while it’s still dark. Stone is gone. She runs and gets Peter and John. They sprint there, look inside, see the empty linen… and go home.  

They leave.  

She stays.  

She’s still crying outside when two angels ask her why. Then she turns around, sees Jesus, and thinks he’s the gardener. She’s so wrecked she’s ready to carry the body herself if he’ll just tell her where it is.  

He says one word. “**Mary**.”  

That’s it. She knows.  

He sends her to go tell the others.  

[soft] The resurrection gets announced by the woman who refused to leave the graveyard.  

[whisper] I’m crying.

## 11. The Scarlet Cord  
`rahab-rope` · Joshua 2 · ~68s

**Hook:**  
She lied to the king, hid the spies, and negotiated her whole family out of a war… [pause]

Two Israelite spies sneak into Jericho and end up at Rahab’s house. The text does not soften what she does for a living.  

The king finds out and sends soldiers. Rahab hides the spies, looks the soldiers dead in the face, and **lies**. Sends them the wrong way.  

Then she goes up to the roof and tells the spies the real reason: “I know your God has given you this land. We heard what happened at the Red Sea and our hearts melted. Your God is God in heaven and on earth.”  

That’s a full confession of faith from a Canaanite sex worker — sharper than most of Israel at the time.  

Then she negotiates… not for herself. For her **whole family**. Father, mother, brothers, sisters.  

Scarlet cord in the window. Her household lives.  

And Matthew puts her in the family line of Jesus.  

[soft] I’m obsessed.

## 12. Milk, Then a Hammer  
`jael-tent` · Judges 4 · ~70s

**Hook:**  
She gave him milk, tucked him in, then finished the war… [pause]

Sisera has been crushing Israel for twenty years with 900 iron chariots. His army finally collapses and he’s running for his life on foot. He ducks into the nearest tent.  

A woman named Jael comes out all soft: “Turn in, my lord. Fear not.”  

He asks for water. She gives him **milk**. Covers him up. He falls asleep.  

Then she takes a tent peg and a hammer… and goes **softly**.  

Walks out, finds the commander hunting him, and says “Come, I’ll show you the man you’re looking for.”  

The victory song calls her blessed above women.  

And it ends with Sisera’s mother waiting at the window wondering why his chariot is taking so long while her friends reassure her he’s just dividing the girls.  

[soft] She served milk and a murder.  

[whisper] I’m scared of her and I love her.

## 13. She Took His ID  
`tamar-veil` · Genesis 38 · ~70s

**Hook:**  
She took his government ID and then almost got burned alive for it… [pause]

Tamar marries Judah’s oldest son. He dies. Next brother. He dies too. Judah has one more son but parks her at her dad’s house “until he’s grown” and then never sends for her. Years pass. She’s stuck with no rights.  

So she puts on a veil, sits by the road, and when Judah walks past he doesn’t recognize her and propositions her.  

She says fine, but I need a pledge. He asks what. She says your signet, your bracelets, and your staff.  

That’s his **government ID**.  

Three months later people tell Judah she’s pregnant and he’s ready to have her burned alive.  

She sends back his own signet and staff with a note that basically says “recognize these?”  

In front of everyone he has to admit: “She hath been more righteous than I.”  

[soft] She played him so clean he had to say it out loud.  

[whisper] I’m on the floor.

## 14. Timbrel on the Shore  
`miriam-song` · Exodus 15 · ~65s

**Hook:**  
The sea just closed on an entire army and she reached for a **tambourine**… [pause]

Israel just walked through the sea on dry ground and watched the water crash down on the entire Egyptian army.  

Moses and the people sing first.  

Then Miriam the prophetess grabs a tambourine. All the women follow her with instruments and dancing. She leads the response: “Sing to the Lord, for he has triumphed gloriously. The horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea.”  

The text calls her two things in one verse: prophetess, and sister of Aaron.  

[soft] When the water closed and the impossible happened… she was the one who reached for an instrument.  

[whisper] I’m soft for that energy.

## 15. I Will Not Leave  
`shunammite` · 2 Kings 4 · ~65s

**Hook:**  
Her son died on her lap and she still said “it shall be well”… [pause]

This wealthy woman notices the prophet Elisha keeps passing through and builds him a whole room — bed, table, chair, lamp. He wants to repay her. She says she’s fine. He gives her a son anyway.  

Years later the boy is out in the field, says “my head, my head,” and dies on her lap at noon.  

She carries him upstairs, lays him on the prophet’s bed, shuts the door, and calls for a donkey.  

Her husband asks why she’s going. She just says “it shall be well.” Doesn’t tell him the child is dead. Doesn’t ask permission.  

She rides to Elisha, blows past his servant, grabs the prophet by the feet and says “as the Lord lives and as your soul lives, I will **not** leave you.”  

He gets up and follows her.  

[soft] Her son died on her lap and she still said “it shall be well” and refused to let go.  

[whisper] I’m in awe.

## 16. Borrow Not a Few  
`widow-oil` · 2 Kings 4 · ~65s

**Hook:**  
The miracle stopped exactly when the jars ran out and that detail is **everything**… [pause]

A widow’s husband dies. The creditor is coming — not for the furniture, for her two sons. They’re about to be taken as slaves to pay the debt.  

Elisha asks her what she has in the house. She says nothing… except a little jar of oil.  

He tells her to go borrow empty vessels from all her neighbors. And then he says the line: “borrow **not a few**.”  

She shuts the door with her sons and starts pouring. The oil keeps coming. Vessel after vessel. Until she asks for one more and her son says there are no more left.  

The oil stops exactly when the containers run out.  

She sells it, pays the debt, keeps her sons, and lives on the rest.  

[soft] The miracle matched the size of her vessels.  

[whisper] I’m soft.

## 17. Twelve Years, One Touch  
`bleeding-woman` · Mark 5 · ~65s

**Hook:**  
She spent everything she had and still got worse… then one touch changed everything… [pause]

Twelve years. Every doctor she could afford. Mark says she “suffered many things of many physicians, and had spent all that she had, and was nothing bettered, but rather grew worse.”  

She’s broke and out of options. She hears about Jesus and decides she’s not even going to ask. She just needs to touch his clothes.  

She comes up behind him in the crowd, touches, and immediately she’s healed.  

She could have disappeared. That was the whole plan.  

But he stops the entire procession and asks “who touched my clothes?” The disciples think he’s crazy — look at this crowd. He keeps looking.  

She comes forward terrified and tells him everything. All twelve years. Out loud.  

He calls her **Daughter** and tells her to go in peace.  

[soft] He wouldn’t let her leave as a secret.  

[whisper] I’m crying.

## 18. She Argued for Crumbs  
`canaanite-mother` · Matthew 15 · ~68s

**Hook:**  
She took Jesus’ own metaphor and **flipped** it on him… [pause]

A Canaanite woman comes after Jesus screaming for help for her demonized daughter.  

He doesn’t answer her. Not one word.  

The disciples get annoyed and tell him to send her away because she’s being loud.  

He says he was only sent to the lost sheep of Israel.  

She still doesn’t leave. She kneels and says “Lord, help me.”  

Then he gives her the hard line about not giving the children’s bread to dogs.  

She doesn’t get offended. She takes his metaphor and flips it: “Truth, Lord… but even the dogs eat the crumbs that fall from the master’s table.”  

He looks at her and says “O woman, great is thy faith.” Her daughter is healed that hour.  

[soft] She argued with Jesus for crumbs and won.  

[whisper] I’m obsessed.

## 19. Decades of Showing Up  
`anna-temple` · Luke 2 · ~68s

**Hook:**  
Eighty-four years of showing up with **zero** proof… and she still got the scoop… [pause]

Anna. Prophetess. From the tribe of Asher — one of the northern tribes that got scattered centuries earlier. Most people had written her people off.  

Married seven years. Then her husband dies. Luke says she was a widow of about eighty-four years. Decades.  

She never left the temple. Fasting and praying night and day. Waiting for something she had no proof was coming.  

One ordinary morning a poor couple walks in with a newborn to do the legal requirement. Nothing about it looks special.  

She walks in at that exact instant, sees the baby, and then goes out and tells everyone in Jerusalem who’s been waiting for redemption.  

[soft] Eighty-four years of nothing… and she got the scoop.  

[whisper] I’m soft for the long game.

## 20. And She Constrained Us  
`lydia-house` · Acts 16 · ~63s

**Hook:**  
She literally strong-armed the apostles into moving into her house… [pause]

Paul and the team get to Philippi. On the Sabbath they go to the river where the women gather to pray.  

There’s Lydia. Seller of purple — luxury fabric. She has money and runs a business.  

The Lord opens her heart. She gets baptized along with her whole household.  

Then she makes an offer that isn’t really an offer: “If you judge me faithful to the Lord, come stay at my house.”  

Luke adds four words that tell you everything: “And she **constrained** us.”  

She would not take no.  

Later in the same chapter, after the earthquake and the jailer’s household getting baptized, where do they go? Back to Lydia’s house.  

Her house became the base of operations.  

[soft] Not asked. Told.  

[whisper] I’m obsessed with her energy.

## 21. She Taught the Teacher  
`priscilla-teach` · Acts 18 · ~63s

**Hook:**  
The most eloquent preacher in the city was only 80% right… so she fixed it… [pause]

A brilliant preacher named Apollos shows up in Ephesus. Eloquent. Mighty in the Scriptures. Teaching diligently… but he only knows the baptism of John. He’s missing the full picture and preaching with total confidence.  

Aquila and Priscilla hear him.  

They don’t correct him publicly. They don’t go home and talk about him.  

They take him aside and explain the way of God more perfectly.  

He goes on to become a massive help to the church.  

And in several places — including this one — Priscilla’s name is listed **first**.  

[soft] She taught the teacher. Quietly. Effectively.  

[whisper] I’m soft for that move.

## 22. Laughter Behind the Tent  
`sarah-laugh` · Genesis 18 · ~65s

**Hook:**  
She laughed silently in her head and still got **caught**… [pause]

Three visitors show up at Abraham’s tent. One of them says Sarah is going to have a son.  

Sarah is listening behind the tent door. The text is blunt — she’s old, past the age.  

She laughs inside herself. Not out loud. And thinks “after I’m worn out and my lord is old, shall I have pleasure?”  

The Lord immediately asks Abraham, “Why did Sarah laugh?”  

Caught. Instantly. Silent laugh behind a tent flap.  

She panics and says “I didn’t laugh.”  

He answers, gently and immovably, “No… but you did.”  

He doesn’t cancel the promise.  

She has the boy. Names him Isaac — which means laughter — and says “God has made me laugh.”  

[whisper] I’m soft for her.

## 23. Thou God Seest Me  
`hagar-seen` · Genesis 16 · ~68s

**Hook:**  
A runaway pregnant slave is the first person in the entire Bible to **name** God… [pause]

Sarai can’t have kids so she gives her Egyptian slave Hagar to Abram like she’s a solution to a problem. Nobody asks Hagar anything.  

Hagar gets pregnant. The tension explodes. Sarai treats her so badly that Hagar runs — pregnant, alone, no plan.  

The angel of the Lord finds her by a random spring in the middle of nowhere and asks “where did you come from and where are you going?”  

She answers the first part. She doesn’t answer the second. She has nowhere to go.  

He tells her to name the boy Ishmael because the Lord has heard her affliction.  

And then Hagar does something no one in Scripture has done yet.  

She names God. “Thou God seest me.”  

[soft] A runaway foreign slave is the first person to give God a name.  

[whisper] I’m not okay.

## 24. Five Sisters Speak Right  
`zelophehad` · Numbers 27 · ~65s

**Hook:**  
Five sisters argued property law in front of the entire nation and God said they were **right**… [pause]

Five sisters. Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, Tirzah. The text lists every single name.  

Their father dies with no sons. Under the current law the land and the family name just disappear.  

These five women don’t petition quietly. They walk up to Moses, the priest, the leaders, and the entire congregation at the door of the tabernacle and make a legal argument: why should our father’s name disappear just because he had no son? Give us an inheritance among our uncles.  

Moses doesn’t wing it. He takes their case straight to the Lord.  

The answer comes back: “The daughters of Zelophehad speak **right**.”  

God agreed with them on the record.  

The inheritance law of Israel got rewritten because five sisters showed up and asked.  

[soft] I’m obsessed.

## 25. They Asked the Prophetess  
`huldah-scroll` · 2 Kings 22 · ~63s

**Hook:**  
The king had a building full of priests… and still sent them to a **woman**… [pause]

They’re renovating the temple and the high priest finds the Book of the Law. Finds it. In the temple. That’s already a scandal.  

They read it to King Josiah. He tears his clothes because he realizes how far the nation has drifted.  

He needs a word from God. There’s a high priest, a scribe, and a whole religious establishment standing right there.  

He sends five men… to Huldah the prophetess.  

A woman. For the authoritative reading of Scripture.  

She doesn’t soften it. She opens with “Thus saith the Lord God of Israel.” Judgment on the nation. Then personal mercy for Josiah because his heart was tender.  

Her reading becomes national policy. The whole reformation runs on what she said.  

[soft] I’m soft for her.

## 26. All Her Living  
`widow-mite` · Mark 12 · ~60s

**Hook:**  
He sat down on purpose just to **watch** people give… [pause]

Jesus sits down opposite the temple treasury on purpose and watches people give.  

Rich people throw in large amounts. That’s in the text.  

Then a poor widow comes and throws in two tiny coins — Mark even converts the currency so you understand how small it is.  

Jesus calls his disciples over like “you need to see this.”  

He says she put in more than all of them combined… because they gave out of their surplus, but she gave out of her poverty. All she had. **All her living**.  

He’s not measuring the size of the gift.  

[soft] He’s measuring what was left afterward.

## 27. Of Their Own Substance  
`joanna-fund` · Luke 8 · ~60s

**Hook:**  
One of the women funding the ministry was married to Herod’s household manager… [pause]

Jesus is traveling and preaching with the twelve. Luke drops three quick verses that most people skip.  

Certain women who had been healed are with them… and he names them. Mary Magdalene. Joanna. Susanna. And many others.  

Stop on Joanna. Luke casually drops that she’s the wife of Chuza, Herod’s household manager.  

That’s inside the royal court. The same court that killed John the Baptist. She has money, access, and **everything** to lose.  

And the line is: they ministered to him of their own substance.  

Their own resources.  

Luke didn’t have to write that. He wanted us to know exactly who was funding the ministry.  

[soft] I’m obsessed with that detail.

## 28. The Coats She Made  
`dorcas-needle` · Acts 9 · ~72s

**Hook:**  
There’s only one woman in the whole New Testament that Luke calls a disciple… and she spent her life **sewing**… [pause]

Tabitha (Dorcas in Greek). Luke describes her in one line: full of good works and the alms she did. Not that she was nice. What she **actually** did.  

She dies. They wash her and put her upstairs. The disciples hear Peter is nearby and send two men begging him to come fast.  

He comes. They take him upstairs. And all the widows are standing there weeping… holding up the coats and clothes she made for them while she was alive.  

That’s the eulogy. Not words. **Evidence**.  

Peter puts them all out, kneels, and prays. Then says “Tabitha, arise.”  

One letter away from the words Jesus used when he raised a little girl in a room Peter was in.  

[soft] She spent her life sewing for widows. And the widows brought the proof.  

[whisper] I’m soft.

## 29. A Succourer of Many  
`phoebe-letter` · Romans 16 · ~63s

**Hook:**  
Paul told an entire church to give this woman **whatever** she asked for… [pause]

Paul opens the last chapter of Romans with a personal commendation: “I commend to you Phoebe our sister, a servant of the church at Cenchrea.”  

That word “servant” is diakonos — the same word translated deacon or minister elsewhere. Translators made a choice.  

He tells the whole Roman church to receive her and help her with whatever she needs… because she has been a succourer (a patron, a protector) of many, including Paul himself.  

She backed him personally.  

And the fact that she’s named first and sent with the letter is why a lot of people believe she carried Romans to Rome. It’s a strong inference, not a direct quote.  

[soft] What is direct: Paul told an entire church to do whatever she asked.  

[whisper] I’m obsessed with her.

## 30. A Year Poured Out  
`mary-perfume` · John 12 · ~68s

**Hook:**  
She poured a year’s wages on the floor… and the man who complained was **stealing**… [pause]

Six days before Passover. Dinner at Bethany. Martha is serving. Lazarus (the one who was dead) is at the table.  

Mary takes a pound of extremely expensive perfume — about a year’s wages — and pours it on Jesus’ feet, then wipes them with her hair. The whole house smells like it.  

Judas immediately starts doing the math out loud: “Why wasn’t this sold for three hundred denarii and given to the poor?”  

On the surface it sounds reasonable.  

John tells us the real motive: he didn’t care about the poor. He was the treasurer and he was **stealing** from the bag.  

Jesus shuts it down immediately: “Leave her alone. She kept this for the day of my burial.”  

He defended her out loud, in the room, in front of the man who was counting.  

[soft] A year’s wages on the floor… and Jesus called it beautiful.  

[whisper] I’m soft for her.
